# El Niño 2026–27: expected damage and our potential impact

As of 7 November 2026. Draft for the SENRI El Niño group. Numbers marked as "our estimate" are
order-of-magnitude reasoning, not data.

## Summary

The 2026–27 El Niño is expected to cost the world about **$1 trillion of GDP in 2027 alone** and
**$7–28 trillion over six years** ([PIIE](https://www.piie.com/blogs/realtime-economics/2026/el-nino-could-drag-down-global-economy-almost-1-trillion-or-least-7),
based on the Callahan & Mankin model). By the end of 2027 about **49 million more people** will be
in acute food insecurity ([WFP via Yale E360](https://e360.yale.edu/features/super-el-nino-agriculture)).
Nobody has published a death estimate. Our rough estimate is **about 100,000 excess deaths (range
35,000–280,000)**, mostly from hunger, disease and heat.

A team of four volunteers will not move these global numbers noticeably: realistically less than
0.01%. Our contribution can only come through leverage. We can help organisations that already
spend money to act earlier and target better, and we can run 1–3 community pilots. In a good
scenario that means influencing $1–5 million of decisions and helping thousands of people in the
pilots.

The window is not closed. The worst hunger is expected in March–August 2027 in East Africa and
November 2027 – March 2028 in Southern Africa
([FEWS NET](https://fews.net/global/special-report/october-2026)). We should therefore measure
ourselves by intermediate indicators (use, partners, speed of matching needs to offers), not by
deaths.

### Our estimate of deaths

Order of magnitude of excess deaths in 2026–2028, by channel. Built by us from historical
benchmarks and current figures; confidence is low.

| Channel | Range, thousand deaths | Reasoning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hunger and malnutrition | 20–150 | +49 M in acute food insecurity; mortality rises mainly where there is conflict (Somalia, Sudan, South Sudan). The 2022 Somalia drought alone caused about 43,000 excess deaths (from memory, VERIFY). |
| Infections (dengue, malaria, cholera, diarrhoea) | 5–50 | WHO rates the risks "very high"; malnutrition makes infections deadlier. |
| Heat, fires and smoke | 5–50 | El Niño adds to global temperature in 2027 on an already high base; WHO cites a record 154,000 deaths from wildfire smoke in 2024. |
| Direct disasters (floods, landslides, storms) | 5–30 | 1997–98: about 23,000; the event is stronger, but warning systems are better. |
| **Total** | **35–280, central about 100** | A count after the event will probably never appear: indirect deaths are rarely counted. |

Illness in the same terms: millions of additional cases. Benchmarks are about 6 million more
underweight children after 2015 and several million dengue cases above normal.

## Success indicators and our potential

The 6 November 2026 call named nine indicators
([recording, 13:52–48:04](https://fathom.video/calls/889662581?timestamp=832)). The first three are
why we do this, but our share in them cannot be measured. The other six we can move and count
ourselves.

| Indicator | What is at stake globally | Our realistic contribution in 6–12 months | How to measure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Avoided losses | ≈$1 tn GDP in 2027 | $0.3–10 M, if our material helps shift $1–5 M of others' money earlier or target it better. Early action returns $1.4–3 per dollar ([Anticipation Hub](https://www.anticipation-hub.org/Documents/Briefing/An_approach_that_works_FINAL.pdf)). | number of decisions and budgets where a partner relied on our material |
| 2. Lives saved | 35–280 thousand excess deaths | from 0 to a few dozen; cannot be proven directly | not a KPI; cite partners' estimates |
| 3. Less suffering and hunger | +49 M in acute food insecurity | indirect; directly hundreds or thousands of people in pilots | people who got help through our tool |
| 4. Share of population with access to what it needs (shelter, medicine, food) | tens of millions in affected areas | only in 1–3 pilot communities of 5–50 thousand people | share of pilot districts with up-to-date resources on the map |
| 5. A need is met quickly by an existing offer | the system has no shared count | our most measurable indicator: tens to hundreds of need–offer pairs in a pilot | share of needs met within 48 hours; median time to match |
| 6. Partners adopt our solutions | anticipatory action frameworks in 75 countries, hundreds of organisations | 1–3 organisations actually use the map, data or MCP | organisations with regular use; links in their documents |
| 7. Money already allocated is used better | ≈$475 M for early action this cycle: CERF up to $100 M, FAO–WFP appeal $202 M, UNICEF $173 M | we touch 0.2–1% of it in a good scenario | partner budgets where our data was used |
| 8. The tool is actually used | — | hundreds of returning users, 5–20 outside contributors | visits and return visits, MCP calls, edits and issues from people outside the team |
| 9. Verified impact | — | 1–5 confirmations from end users or partners | a short case with a quote and a number; the basis for microgrants |

### Three scenarios

| Scenario | What happens | Money influenced | People | Lives (estimate) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Base | the map and research stay a reference for us and a few readers | ≈$0 | — | 0 |
| Good | one organisation uses our data; 1–2 pilots matching needs and offers | $1–5 M | thousands | ones to dozens |
| Best | a network such as Anticipation Hub, Start Network or the Red Cross Climate Centre builds our tool into its work | $10–50 M | tens of thousands | hundreds |

How lives were counted: early action gives $0.3–2 of net benefit per dollar shifted, 10–20% of that
benefit can be attributed to us (partners do the work), and a life saved costs $5–50 thousand. The
share and the cost per life are our assumptions, not sourced, so the table shows the middle of a
wide range. Even the best scenario is less than 0.1% of the global damage.

Our leverage is strongest on problem-map items that need neither money nor field presence: 5.3
(fragmented data), 5.5 (no check that early action worked), 3.7 (thin evidence on
cost-effectiveness), 7.1 (fragmented mandates) and X5 (lessons decay between events).

## Assumptions and what to check

- All GDP estimates (PIIE, Allianz, $5.7 tn for 1997–98) come from one model, Callahan & Mankin;
  they are not independent sources. They count lost growth, not destroyed property.
- An estimate of $451 bn (India, US and China about 60% of losses) has circulated in the press since
  October 2026, but we could not find the original report, so it is left out of the table.
- The death estimate and all figures on our impact are our calculations, not data. The 2022 Somalia
  figure is from memory and needs checking.
- The "23,000 deaths in 1997–98" figure is widely cited but counts mostly direct deaths; deaths from
  hunger and disease are largely missing from it.
- The different counts of people (8.8 M in the FAO–WFP appeal, 49 M more in hunger, 162 M children
  at risk) measure different things and do not add up.
- Update this file when the next FEWS NET report appears (expected late 2026) and when PAHO and
  WHO publish new situation reports.
- For indicators 5, 8 and 9, build data collection into the tools now (counters, timestamps on needs
  and offers); otherwise there will be nothing to show funders later.
